📜 Methodology — the STOCK Act edge

Members of Congress write the laws, budgets, and regulations that move entire industries — and they trade the very same stocks. The Congress Index turns their legally-disclosed trades into a rules-based, copyable strategy and marks it against the real S&P 500.

🏛️ The STOCK Act of 2012

Before 2012, congressional trading was a black box. The Stop Trading on Congressional Knowledge (STOCK) Act banned trading on non-public information gained through the job and — critically — forced members to publicly disclose every trade within 45 days. Those official filings are the raw material for this index. That same 45-day lag is why this is a research tool, not a way to front-run anyone.

📈 Why it's an edge

Members sit on committees that draft legislation, receive closed-door and industry briefings, and see regulatory direction months ahead of the public. The biggest wins cluster in the exact sectors they oversee — defense, health, energy, tech. Peer-reviewed studies of Senate and House portfolios have documented statistically significant, market-beating returns for years.

How the index is built — the exact rules
  • Universe: STOCK Act era only — every position is a trade disclosed from 2012 onward.
  • Selection: at each rebalance, members are ranked by trailing total return over the prior lookback window (default 2 years) with a minimum trade cadence. The "top N" is only taken when the qualifying pool is at least twice N — so it's a genuine selection, not just whoever happened to trade.
  • Weighting: by trade size, equal, or a multi-factor Score = Alpha (return above the S&P) + Consistency (win rate vs the S&P) + Committee (share of trades whose sector overlaps a committee the member sits on).
  • Measurement: returns are marked to market between non-overlapping rebalance dates so they chain legitimately; per-period moves are winsorized at +150% / −95% to tame delisted and mis-matched tickers; when a member leaves office their positions close at their last-served price.
  • Benchmark: the real S&P 500 (SPY) over the identical dates.
